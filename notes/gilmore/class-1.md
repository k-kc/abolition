# Class Plan: Bibliography Workshop + Semester Check-In + Gilmore

**Format:** Small seminar (~10 students) | **Total time:** 3 hours
**Anchoring texts:** Gilmore interview (primary) / Wacquant (supplemental)

---

## Block 1 — Annotated Bibliography Workshop (40 min)

**Quick framing (5 min)**
Tell students: an annotated bibliography isn't just a list — it's an argument about *why* each source belongs. Each entry should do two things: describe what the source does and explain why *you* need it.

**Individual writing (10 min)**
Each student picks one source from their proposal bibliography and writes a draft annotation on the spot. Prompt them with two questions:
- What does this source actually *do* — what's its argument or contribution?
- Why do *you* need it — what would be missing from your project without it?

**Pair workshop (15 min)**
Partners swap annotations and give feedback using Belcher's four categories as a diagnostic:
- Did the annotation make clear which category this source belongs to?
- Did it explain the source's argument, or just describe its topic?
- Is the "why I need it" specific to this project, or could it belong to anyone's bibliography?

**Whole group (10 min)**
Surface one or two examples where the category was genuinely unclear or where the annotation described a topic instead of an argument. Hold the tension rather than resolving it — that's the work.

---

## Block 2 — Where We've Been (35 min)

**Individual (5 min, written)**
Each student writes down:
- One concept, framework, or argument from the first half of the semester they feel like they actually *have*
- One question they're still sitting with that the readings haven't resolved

**Small groups of 3–4 (15 min)**
Share and look for overlap and disagreement. Where do people feel solid? Where do the same questions keep surfacing across different projects? Does the group agree on what they have, or is that itself contested?

**Whole group (15 min)**
Map it on the board: *what we've built* on one side, *what's still open* on the other. Pay particular attention to tensions that have persisted across multiple weeks — Wacquant vs. Gilmore is likely to appear here already. Don't close the open questions. Let them hang as the frame for the rest of the semester and the discussion that follows.

## Block 3 — Gilmore Discussion with Wacquant as Supplement (90 min)

**Opening anchor (5 min)**
Pose this to the room before discussion begins:
> Is the prison a product of capitalism, or a tool of it?

Let a few quick responses land without elaboration — you'll return to this at the end.

**Discussion spine (~65 min)**

1. *Surface the accounts:* Gilmore traces the prison back to disciplining workers to the wage — the stevedore, the tobacco, the death penalty for theft. Wacquant locates the carceral expansion in the collapse of the Fordist compact and the rise of workfare. In your own words, what is each argument? Where do they agree, and where do they diverge?

2. *Test Gilmore:* Gilmore says organized abandonment created a vacuum, and the prison rose up to fill it. But is that a story about capitalism *producing* the prison, or about the state *choosing* it as a response? Does her account actually answer the anchor question — or does it sidestep it?

3. *Test Wacquant:* Wacquant argues workfare and prisonfare work in tandem to enforce the discipline of desocialized wage labor — which sounds like a tool argument. But he also insists the penal state is *productive*, not just repressive. Does that complicate the tool framing? Can something be both a tool and generative of new realities?

4. *Push both:* Neither Gilmore nor Wacquant spends much time on who *decides* — which actors, institutions, or class fractions are reaching for the prison and why. Does that gap matter? What would the analysis look like if we centered decision and agency rather than structure?

**Closing return (20 min)**
Come back to the opening question: *Is the prison a product of capitalism, or a tool of it?*

Has the answer changed? Let students revise or complicate their opening responses. Push on anyone who wants to say "both" — both in what proportion, and does it matter for what we do next? Don't synthesize — let the disagreement stay visible.

---

## Exits (5 min)

Each student leaves with one written sentence:
> "The question my project still needs to answer is \_\_\_\_."

Collect these — they're useful for upcoming one-on-ones.

---

**Total: 3 hours**
Block 1: 40 min | Block 2: 35 min | Break: 10 min | Block 3: 90 min | Exits: 5 min
