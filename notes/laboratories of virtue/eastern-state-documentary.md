## Historical Context: The Problem of Disorder

### Social and Economic Disruption (@6 mins)
"Problems of crime and disorder alarmed many in the new republic. Political and economic changes were disrupting previous social arrangements, weakening the relationships that had defined and maintained an orderly society. Growing numbers of landless unskilled and unapprenticed people both immigrant and native born traveled the countryside and congregated in newly emerging cities...concern about the undisciplined, immoral, and illegal conduct of these lower classes. Establishing social order among the poor was a prevailing concern."

## Two Prison Systems of the 1820s

### Auburn: The Silent System (New York)

#### Core Approach
- Congregate prison discipline
- Flogging to prohibit conversation
- Maintenance of silence

#### Human Cost
Maintaining silence meant sacrificing the humanitarian ideal and abandoned hope and responsibility for reforming criminals.

#### Why It Prevailed
Auburn became the blueprint because its labor was more productive and profitable, which subsidized the cost of the institution.

---

### Pennsylvania: The Separate System of Discipline

#### Philosophical Foundation
The Pennsylvania Prison Society rejected the brutality necessary to maintain the Auburn System.

#### Reformative Vision (@10:30)
"This mode of punishment weighing as it does upon the hardened and impenitent spirit is eminently calculated to break down his obdurate spirit. when that important object of penitentiary discipline has been gained he learns and he feels that moral and religious reflection relieved by industrious occupation at his trade comfort and support his mental and physical powers, divest his cell of all its horrors and its punishment of much of its severity." (Cox, 1837)

#### Cost
It was the most expensive public building yet constructed in the United States.

## Who Filled the Prisons

### Population (@23:00)
The majority of men and women who served time at Eastern were sentenced for property crimes and came from the lower economic classes.

### Social Engineering and Class Anxiety
"The invention of penitentiary represented optimism about the possibilities of social engineering and anxieties about the emerging lower classes. That anxiety about the poor proved an enduring motivation for the reform and expansion of the prison system."

### Nativism and Blame (@24:30)
"Native born whites portrayed crime as a problem brought to America by outsiders. 'Had the native population of our country been left the natural and potent influence of these conditions we should have an unprecedented minimum of crime. indeed no one familiar with our criminal return can fail to see how large a percentage of convicts are of foreign born or extraction.'"

## Critique of Eastern State

### Practical Failures
- Complaints about expense involved with building and maintaining the prison
- Solitary labor unproductive and unprofitable
- Prisoners didn't learn discipline

### Systemic Abuses
State investigation charged:
- Prisoner abuse
- Embezzlements
- Immoral actions
  - Prisoners allowed out of cells to work for staff
  - Lax enforcement of separation
  - Pairing prisoners to learn a craft or trade (violating solitude principle)
  - Use of severe and abusive punishments against prisoners
    - 42 days of isolation
    - By the early 1900s, solitary became a punishment tool

### Fundamental Inadequacy (@32)
"But the granite walls and iron bars, though they deprive the prisoner of his liberty and inflict a just physical punishment, do not work that reformation in the soul of the man that will restore him to society regenerated and reformed. Until this is in some measure accomplished, our system of prison discipline is imperfect and ineffectual" (Chairman of the First National Congress on the Penitentiary)

## The Irish System: An Alternative Model

### Progressive Philosophy
- Prisoners progress through stages of increasing freedom
- Leading to conditional release/parole
- Contrary to Enlightenment thinkers' approach

### New Theoretical Basis
Flawed individuals are the result of flawed environments; they need no punishment but active reformation guided by the new sciences of human behavior.

### Humanizing Reforms Attempted
- Incorporated teachers
- Libraries
- Visits and letters with family

### Structural Mismatch
The design of the prison meant that it wasn't well suited to these humanizing reforms.

### Problem of Expansion
Increasing the number of prisons

#### Persistent Question
How is it the prisons are always overcrowded???
